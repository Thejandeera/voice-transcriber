Sentiment analysis is a method used to identify the emotion or opinion expressed in text or speech. It is often used to determine whether a message is positive, negative, or neutral. For example, if a customer says that a service was excellent and helpful, the system may classify the statement as positive. If the customer says that the service was slow or disappointing, the system may classify it as negative. A statement that only provides information without showing a clear emotion may be classified as neutral.
Sentiment analysis is commonly used in customer service, social media monitoring, product reviews, surveys, and call-center systems. It helps organizations understand how people feel about a product, service, or experience. Instead of manually reading thousands of comments or listening to many recorded calls, a computer system can analyze the content and produce useful results more quickly.
In a FreePBX project, sentiment analysis can be added after a call recording has been converted into text. For example, Whisper can first transcribe the recorded telephone conversation. The transcription can then be sent to a sentiment analysis model. The model may examine the words, phrases, and overall tone of the conversation and return a result such as positive, negative, or neutral.
This feature can be useful for identifying calls that may require attention. A strongly negative call may show that a customer is unhappy, confused, or frustrated. A positive call may show that the customer was satisfied with the service. Neutral calls may include general questions, simple requests, or information-sharing conversations.
Sentiment analysis can also provide a confidence score. For example, the system may report that a call is negative with eighty-five percent confidence. This score shows how certain the model is about its decision. The result could be stored with the Call Detail Record and displayed through the backend API.
A dashboard could show the number of positive, negative, and neutral calls for a selected day or period. This would help an organization monitor customer satisfaction and identify common problems. Negative calls could also be highlighted so that a supervisor can review the recording or transcription.
However, sentiment analysis is not always completely accurate. Sarcasm, jokes, mixed emotions, unclear language, and cultural differences can make the result difficult to classify. A person may also sound calm while using negative words, or sound frustrated while making a reasonable request. For this reason, sentiment analysis should be used as a supporting tool rather than the only method for making important decisions.
Privacy is also important because call recordings and transcriptions may contain personal or sensitive information. Access should be limited, and the data should be stored securely. Overall, sentiment analysis can make a FreePBX call-management system more intelligent by helping users understand the emotional tone of recorded conversations.
